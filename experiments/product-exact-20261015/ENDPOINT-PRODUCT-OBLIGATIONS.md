# Written and restored control products: source census scope

The fixed census reads the frozen original95 stream. It compares a product at
a selected use with the product of those same physical controls at the next
top-level cleanup boundary, within one conservative immutable-ID symbolic
state. The separately established repeated-group identity is joined through
that same indexed use. No symbolic IDs are compared across independent passes.

A positive endpoint count is only a proof lead. Constants, unreachable guards,
existing first-use suppression, interval conflicts and absent zero donors may
make every recovered endpoint unprofitable. No executed-Toffoli mean follows
from this structural census. A zero recovered count ends this approach.

Any future candidate would need a separately admitted renderer and independent
checker establishing all of the following on the exact source and output:

- Every replaced use has the same Boolean control product as the cached value,
  under all inputs and original measurement assignments. Matching a physical
  control pair or its final version numbers alone is insufficient.
- The donor starts at zero, is disjoint from every live operand and is untouched
  between allowed cache operations; it is restored at the lease endpoint.
- First-active-use flags reproduce every original classical guard, including
  nested scopes and mutable guard bits. Private flags are cleaned.
- At cleanup the current physical control product equals the cached value.
  This permits the usual measurement/CZ cleanup lemma even if individual
  controls were written, swapped or restored during the lease. A numerical
  replay cannot replace this universal endpoint obligation.
- The complete emitted instruction sequence, added measurement projection,
  phase behavior and original classical outputs obey the existing cache
  theorem. No raw-write fence is relaxed without the new endpoint proof.

The root agent owns the separate global-cleanup/snapshot renderer and proposal
census. This source census does not authorize or implement that method.
